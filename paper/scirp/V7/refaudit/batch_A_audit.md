## Citing-context issues

### MISCITED (text fix needed)

1. **articlebrown (GPT-3 should be GPT-2).** The paper says "...along with the GPT-3 model \cite{articlebrown}...". The model benchmarked was GPT-2 (`gpt2`, 117M; Table 2 and `GPT2_MODEL = 'gpt2'`).
   **Fix:** "...along with the GPT-2 model \cite{radford2019language}...". The same error appears in the "Transformer LM DL Testing" list item ("...BERT, RoBERTa, DistilBERT, GPT-3, XLNet..."); change GPT-3 to GPT-2 there too. You can drop `articlebrown`, or keep it only if GPT-3 is discussed as background.

2. **articlebi (wrong DeepSeek paper for the model used).** The paper says "...smaller language models from both the Llama \cite{touvron2023llama} and DeepSeek family \cite{articlebi}, were included in the benchmarking...". The DeepSeek model actually used is `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B`, which was released with DeepSeek-R1.
   **Fix:** cite `\cite{guo2025deepseek}` here. In the other sentence ("known for their efficiency and reasoning capabilities ... \cite{articlebi, guo2025deepseek}"), `articlebi` can stay as a citation for the model family. Note that the reasoning claim is supported by `guo2025deepseek`, not by `articlebi`.

3. **articlebai (how the distilled DeepSeek model was made).** The paper says "In our evaluation, we also include a distilled DeepSeek model based on techniques similar to those used for optimizing Qwen models \cite{articlebai, hinton2015distilling}." This is inaccurate. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B *is* a Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B base model, fine-tuned (SFT) on about 800k samples generated by DeepSeek-R1. The 2023 Qwen report covers the first-generation Qwen models, not Qwen2.5.
   **Fix:** "We also include DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, a Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B base model \cite{yang2024qwen25} fine-tuned on reasoning traces generated by DeepSeek-R1 \cite{guo2025deepseek}."
   `yang2024qwen25` is already proposed in batch D. The more specific Qwen2.5-Math report is arXiv:2409.12122 if you prefer it.

4. **articleboja (fastText model and date).**
   - (a) The paper says "...the FastText \cite{articleboja} model we use is crawl-300d-2M.vec.bin, which extends Word2Vec by incorporating subword information, enabling generalization to out-of-vocabulary words." fastText's page lists `crawl-300d-2M.vec` as "2 million word vectors trained on Common Crawl (600B tokens)", which is the variant *without* subword information.
     **Fix:** "...the fastText model we use is crawl-300d-2M (2M word vectors trained on Common Crawl, 600B tokens) \cite{mikolov2018advances}; note that this release does not use the subword (character n-gram) information introduced in \cite{articleboja}."
     The new key would need this entry: Mikolov, Grave, Bojanowski, Puhrsch and Joulin, "Advances in Pre-Training Distributed Word Representations", *Proc. LREC 2018*, ELRA, https://aclanthology.org/L18-1008/.
     Table 2's FastText row says "Embed+Subword"; correct it the same way.
   - (b) The paper says "FastText ... first introduced in 2017 by Meta's AI team".
     **Fix:** "introduced by Facebook AI Research (now Meta AI) in 2016 (arXiv:1607.04606; published in TACL in 2017)".

5. **mikolov (Word2Vec dates and claims).**
   - (a) The paper says "Language models are a technological advancement first introduced in 2012 \cite{mikolov}". The paper is from 2013, and language models predate it by decades: statistical n-gram LMs, then neural LMs such as Bengio et al. 2003.
     **Fix:** "Neural word-embedding models, popularized by Word2Vec in 2013 \cite{mikolov}, provided a more effective and efficient means of representing language...".
   - (b) The paper says "Word embeddings were first introduced in 2013 with Word2Vec \cite{mikolov}". Distributed word representations predate Word2Vec (e.g. Bengio et al., JMLR 2003; Collobert and Weston, ICML 2008).
     **Fix:** "Word embeddings were popularized in 2013 by Word2Vec \cite{mikolov}".
   - (c) The paper says "GoogleNews-vectors-negative300.bin, was trained on Google News data using CBOW and skip-gram methods with negative sampling". Negative sampling and the phrase model behind the released GoogleNews vectors come from Mikolov et al. 2013b, not from this paper. The project page describes the release as "trained on part of Google News dataset (about 100 billion words)" with "300-dimensional vectors for 3 million words and phrases".
     **Fix:** "...trained on about 100 billion words of Google News \cite{mikolov, mikolov2013distributed}".
     Add this entry: Mikolov, Sutskever, Chen, Corrado and Dean, "Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases and their Compositionality", *Advances in NeurIPS 26*, pp. 3111--3119, 2013. The release is a single model, so "using CBOW and skip-gram" should also be dropped.

6. **pennington (training corpora).** The paper says "These embeddings were trained on the Common Crawl dataset ... consisting of over 600 billion tokens." That is only true of the fastText vectors. GloVe 840B is Common Crawl with 840B tokens (2.2M vocabulary, cased), and Word2Vec is Google News with about 100B words.
   **Fix:** "The GloVe (glove.840B.300d; Common Crawl, 840B tokens) and fastText (crawl-300d-2M; Common Crawl, 600B tokens) vectors were trained on Common Crawl, while the Word2Vec vectors were trained on Google News (about 100B words)."

7. **schuster (where WordPiece came from).** The paper says "WordPiece is a subword tokenization algorithm originally developed for Google's neural machine translation system \cite{schuster} \cite{wu}".
   **Fix:** "WordPiece is a subword tokenization algorithm originally developed for Japanese and Korean voice search \cite{schuster}, later adopted in Google's neural machine translation system \cite{wu} and then in BERT \cite{devlin-etal-2019-bert}."

### Minor wording notes (entries are OK; optional fixes)

- **devlin-etal-2019-bert.** The paper says "BERT ... which was introduced in 2019" and "...released in 2019". BERT was released as arXiv:1810.04805 in October 2018 and published at NAACL-HLT in 2019. Suggest "introduced in 2018 (published 2019)", which also keeps the ELMo (2018) and BERT order accurate.
- **sparckjones1972statistical.** The paper says "TF-IDF ... was introduced in 1972". Sparck Jones (1972) introduced the term-specificity (IDF) weighting, and combined tf-idf weighting came out of later SMART-group work. Suggest "whose IDF component was introduced in 1972".
- **chomsky65aspects.** "generational grammars" should read "generative (transformational) grammar". The sentence before it also has a typo: "in the and the book".
- **Chomsky57.** Saying *Syntactic Structures* is the basis of the hierarchy is acceptable. The hierarchy itself was formalised in the 1956 paper (`chomsky56`) and in Chomsky, "On certain formal properties of grammars", *Information and Control* 2(2):137--167, 1959 (doi 10.1016/S0019-9958(59)90362-6). Consider citing the 1959 paper if the hierarchy is the point.
- **deerwester.** "LSA ... leverages matrix eigendecomposition" should say "truncated singular value decomposition (SVD)", which is what Deerwester et al. use and what the paper itself says later. The same sentence has the typo "Principle Component Analysis"; it should be "Principal".
- **salton1971smart.** For "vector space models", consider also citing Salton, Wong and Yang, "A vector space model for automatic indexing", *Commun. ACM* 18(11):613--620, 1975 (doi 10.1145/361219.361220).

### Adjacent issues in the same sentences (keys not in this batch; for whoever audits them)

- **ouyang2022training.** The paper says "DeepSeek models utilize reinforcement learning without preliminary SFT \cite{ouyang2022training}". InstructGPT uses SFT followed by RLHF. RL without preliminary SFT is DeepSeek-R1-Zero, so cite `guo2025deepseek`.
- **touvron2023llama.** This is the LLaMA (v1, February 2023) paper, but the model benchmarked is `meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B`. Consider "The Llama 3 Herd of Models", arXiv:2407.21783 (2024).
- **hinton2015distilling.** The R1-Distill models are SFT on teacher-generated samples, not logit-matching knowledge distillation. Hinton et al. works as background on distillation only.
